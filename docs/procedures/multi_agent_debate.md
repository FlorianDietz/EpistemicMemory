# Running a Multi-Agent Debate

(This is an advanced procedure for high-stakes decisions; most projects won't need it in their first weeks. It is written for the orchestrating agent, not for human reading.)

Escalation ladder first: for most questions, 2–4 independent agents answering blind + comparing their answers is cheaper and often as good — debate at matched compute is not reliably better than independent takes, so the ladder is about correctness as much as cost (a full debate runs roughly 5× a direct answer). Climb to a full debate only when the independent answers materially disagree, the stakes justify it, or the task is option-GENERATION rather than answer-selection — interaction can produce options no blind entry contains, which independent takes cannot.

**Setup:**
1. Create `convos/{user}/{date}_{topic}_DEBATE_LOG.md` — the agents-only transcript (capitalized so agent-internal files stand out in a listing; the user is not expected to read it). At its top, write: the debate's goals, stopping criteria, budget (max debaters/rounds), and **which ordinary LOG spawned this debate** — that spawning LOG is where questions and results are written back for the user (see "User interaction" below). If the goals are unclear, ask the user first — and ask again mid-debate if new information challenges the goals' assumptions.
2. Assemble the input pack (files debaters must read). For substantial debates it includes a **shared summary/inventory** of the material, written by the orchestrator or a dedicated sub-agent before round 0 — debaters treat it as a map, not an authority. The summary must be written TO THE DECISION, not just to identify items: first name what decision the debate serves, derive the fields that would change a verdict if wrong, and state that field spec at the top of the pack (so the gate in step 3 has an explicit target). For a prioritization debate the fields are: the user's own priority/effort words **quoted verbatim**, status with its date and basis, synergies with other candidates, and time-sensitivity; for other debate types, derive the analogous list. These are exactly what one-pass compression systematically drops. Prefer giving different debaters different primary sources to be responsible for — diversity of *inputs* produces more real disagreement than diversity of personas.
3. **Gate every compressed input before round 0.** Compression is this protocol's most-demonstrated failure point: wrong or thinned one-liners cause *silence* (items nobody argues about), and every claim-triggered check downstream is blind to silence. Before issuing round-0 prompts, run 2 auditor sub-agents — on a different model tier than whoever wrote the pack, and never the author itself — each on 3–5 DISJOINT items sampled from the pack's tail (the low-priority/killed end; if the pack is unranked, sample uniformly at random). Each auditor reads its items' primary sources cold and writes what the decision-relevant fields (step 2's list) should say BEFORE looking at the pack's entry, then diffs. Severity: **MATERIAL** = the item's likely verdict would change if the field were corrected; **MINOR** = presentation-level; **NONE**. **One MATERIAL hit fails the whole pack**: this failure mode is systematic (a mis-aimed compression objective), not per-item noise — regenerate the entire pack against the field spec and re-audit a fresh sample; do not just patch the caught item. The gate covers **every compressed decision-input artifact debaters consume** — the shared pack, per-slice distillates, evidence extracts. Honest limits, state them in the DEBATE_LOG: the sample size is an unvalidated default, and auditors sharing the pack author's training priors may reproduce its omissions instead of catching them — the gate is risk reduction plus deterrence, not a guarantee. Record its catch rate (MATERIAL hits ÷ items audited, pooled across auditors) in the outcome.
4. Use 3–4 debaters (3 balances coordination cost against diversity of coverage; going past 4 adds noise faster than insight). Prefer different models where your harness allows it (sub-agents typically inherit the session model unless the spawn specifies otherwise); if same-model, assign different reasoning methods (e.g. first-principles derivation vs. source-driven/empirical vs. adversarial stress-testing), not personality personas.
5. **Two-tier variant — a scaling fallback, not a default.** Trigger: the corpus has outgrown slices — no assignment of overlapping per-debater slices can put every load-bearing source in front of at least one debater — or the step-3 gate itself caught a MATERIAL omission (evidence that compression is failing and debaters must reach primaries directly). When triggered: 2–3 **deep readers**, each reading all — or an overlapping-but-distinct large subset — of the primary sources (a full-corpus read requires corpus ≤ ~half the reader's context window, leaving room for transcript and output), plus 1–2 **fresh reviewers**, respawned each discussion round, working from the gated summary. Deep readers MAY be continued across turns instead of respawned, to avoid re-reading the corpus — but a continued instance defends its own earlier turns more (the reviewer tier exists to counter exactly that), so continuation needs a stop condition: stop continuing an instance when it mostly defends its own prior turns instead of adding new checkable material; respawn fresh from the transcript instead. If your harness cannot continue sub-agent conversations, deep readers respawn fresh per turn and re-read (affordable for mid-size corpora) or work from gated per-slice distillates. Prefer 2 reviewers on distinct models; with only 1, rotate its model each round. **Both tiers write blind round 0 and blind finals.** In round 0, deep readers also amend the shared summary (corrections + omissions, as evidence all debaters read). Reviewer per-turn duty: spot-verify one load-bearing deep-reader claim by reading its cited primary source — a sampled deterrent, not exhaustive coverage. Tier labels NEVER appear in the transcript (an "expert" byline is an authority cue); tier assignments are recorded at the end, with the model mapping in the results report (turn mechanics step 5).

**Rules in every debater's instructions:**
- All agents have equal standing; no role name carries authority (never a designated "fact-checker" trusted by name). Every agent verifies the load-bearing claims it relies on against primary sources; when a claim fails verification, mark it unverified — never invent a replacement fact.
- Roles, if any, are non-binding lenses to spread coverage — never opinions to defend. You are collaborating; you have no fixed position; change your mind the moment a better argument appears — but only by citing the specific new argument or evidence that changed it, never because "the others disagree."
- Per-turn structure requirement (turns are bounded by required structure, not by word caps — truncating agent output only destroys information): every turn must contain (a) at least one new checkable claim or concrete piece of evidence with source + location, (b) engagement with at least one specific prior point, (c) one named way the current front-runner answer could be wrong, and (d) explicit "unverified" markings on any from-memory claim. (b) and (c) apply to discussion turns; blind round 0 and blind finals require only (a) and (d). Genuine agreement is fine to state; don't manufacture dissent to look thorough, and don't add content-free assent.
- The orchestrator may include in a debater's instructions a brief warning about the AI-specific failure modes these rules guard against — sycophantic convergence, anchoring on in-context positions, literal trust in role labels, cheap content-free agreement or dissent — when it judges the warning will help that debater rather than prime it.
- Every debater prompt, from round 0 on, states: **"Do NOT write to any file — return your text to the orchestrator"** (load-bearing, never drop it: debater sub-agents auto-receive CLAUDE.md, whose communication protocol otherwise instructs them to write to LOG files), **"Read only the files listed in this prompt"** (protects blinding and the orchestrator-private file), and **"Do not state your model, tier, or role in your entry"** (the orchestrator otherwise has to redact it to keep the transcript anonymous). The prompt must also carry or name all context the debater needs — sub-agents do NOT auto-load README.

**Turn mechanics — the orchestrator is the single writer (this is what makes shared-file logging safe):**
1. Round 0, blind: each debater independently writes its load-bearing evidence and initial position WITHOUT seeing the others. The orchestrator appends all of them to the debate LOG at once, labeled with neutral IDs (Agent A/B/C — no model or role names in the transcript).
2. Discussion rounds, sequential: spawn each debater FRESH per turn (do not continue its conversation); it reads the transcript as work by *previous agents*. In the two-tier variant, deep readers may be the exception (setup step 5); everything else in the turn mechanics applies to both tiers alike.
3. **Discussion rounds are not a default — there is no minimum.** After round 0, and after every round, add a discussion round if and only if the between-round note names specific new checkable material to discuss: material disagreement between blind entries, a failed verification, or a generative task where positions could be combined into options no blind entry contains. If round 0 already converged, go straight to finals. The stop signal is a round adding no new checkable claims; expect quality to decline within 2–3 rounds **per scoped question** — past the peak, discussion reinforces wrong consensus; never extend to force agreement. For exhaustive claim-checking, never add discussion rounds — run a parallel verification pass instead (independent fresh verifiers, one claim each, no interaction) between the last discussion round and finals. **Falsifier-appropriateness check:** for every named falsifier in a surviving position ("we'll know we're wrong if X"), one verifier confirms the falsifier is appropriate — a live, observable possibility whose occurrence would genuinely falsify the position; inappropriate falsifiers include events that already occurred or were cancelled, and conditions too vague to ever trigger. **Every verification entry must quote the passage it checked verbatim with its location; an entry without the quote is itself marked unverified** ("verified" without evidence is an authority cue, and a verifier that misread a source would otherwise inject a wrong result finals treat as settled). When a verifier cannot find or match a cited source, record it as **UNVERIFIED-MISS** in the outcome with the reason — silence never reads as success. Verifier outputs are appended to the transcript before finals; finals weigh them as evidence about specific claims, never as a scoreboard of positions. **Stress-test variant:** to pressure-test a load-bearing conclusion, the orchestrator may spawn a deliberately rigid advocate instructed NOT to concede; its claims are resolved by the same verification machinery, and its output enters the transcript as evidence for the finals — it does NOT write a final of its own (an unyielding participant is an information-pressure tool only where an independent decision step absorbs it; as a voting debater it would poison the blind-finals aggregation). If a round uncovers a genuinely NEW dimension of the problem (not more argument on the same one), extending is the wrong tool: the orchestrator re-scopes — amend the goals section and run a fresh blind round on the new dimension, or schedule a follow-up debate.
4. Finals, blind: each debater writes its final position without seeing the other finals. Agreement between independent finals is real signal; agreement negotiated during discussion is not.
5. The orchestrator writes the outcome section in the DEBATE_LOG — best current answer(s); preserved disagreements (do NOT synthesize them away); the **weakest link** (the single claim that, if wrong, flips the conclusion); a note that post-convergence confidence is inflated; **tail-completeness: an explicit verdict (adopt / drop / defer / out-of-scope, each with a reason) for EVERY input-pack item** — silent omission is the one failure claim-triggered verification can never catch; the gate's catch rate and the debate's cost (debater calls; token totals if your harness reports them) as metadata — and appends the user-facing results report to the spawning LOG, **including the agent→model mapping and, in the two-tier variant, tier assignments** (kept out of the transcript to avoid biasing debaters; recording them in the spawning LOG keeps the debate verifiable).

**User interaction (sparse, asynchronous) — write back to the spawning LOG:** the user never needs to read the DEBATE_LOG; their interface is the ordinary topic LOG that spawned the debate (normal LOG conventions: append-only, answers inline with `C:`, nothing rewritten — so user responses persist by construction; one LOG can spawn several debates, and they all report back to it). The orchestrator appends there:
- *Between rounds:* the current open questions, each tagged **[blocking]** (debate paused on it) or **[semi-blocking]** (an early preference would prune branches; meanwhile the debate continues on the questions not gated by the answer, and debaters may argue the gated question conditionally on the possible answers). Each new question section starts with a one-line status and says explicitly which earlier questions are now answered or [superseded] — so reading the newest section alone gives the current state.
- *At the end:* the results report — verdict per goal, preserved disagreements, weakest link, recommended actions, and any questions that remain open. **Write the report for the user, not for agents:** plain prose; no inventory IDs, agent letters, or file-shorthand without an in-place one-clause reminder of what the thing is; assume the user recalls topics only when given a hint and has not read the files the debate read.
When a user answer feeds back into the debate, quote it **verbatim** in the DEBATE_LOG or point the debaters at the spawning LOG — never paraphrase-and-discard the original (paraphrase drift is the same failure mode the Quantitative Claims rule targets).

**Pause/resume:** a debate can outlive its orchestrator's session ([blocking] waits, context death). Keep a third per-debate file, `{date}_{topic}_DEBATE_ORCHESTRATOR.md` — header: orchestrator-private, debaters must never read or be pointed at it (protection is instruction-based, so keep the most leak-sensitive content, such as blind entries collected but not yet published to the transcript, there only as long as needed). It holds what a successor orchestrator needs — the test is *can a successor re-create every debater's initialization exactly, and does it know what must stay hidden from whom*: active scoped question(s) and round position; per-debater init specs (model, input pack, prompt template); the agent→model map and who-read-what map; material deliberately withheld from specific debaters; verification assignments and outcomes; posted user questions still unanswered. Do NOT have debaters write private handover notes — anything worth handing over belongs in the transcript.

**Orchestrator duties while refereeing:** watch for one agent's points dominating everything, agents not engaging each other's specifics (rubber-stamping), and convergence without new evidence (conformity cascade). Flag any of these in the outcome section — a debate that converged through conformity is a failed debate even if the answer looks right. The orchestrator referees process; it does not add its own opinion as a hidden extra debater. Between-round notes are written into the DEBATE_LOG — debaters read them as part of the transcript — and follow a fixed factual template (interpretive framing steers the next round's fresh agents): (a) what converged and what did not, with transcript locations; (b) verification results with their verbatim-quote status; (c) the who-read-what map update — the orchestrator is the only participant who knows which debater has seen which sources, so when an argument rests on material its audience hasn't read, say so here, factually; (d) open user questions; (e) any round-extension justification naming its new checkable material. Have a fresh sub-agent check the note against the transcript for interpretive language before debaters see it (cheap; skip only under time pressure, and say so in the outcome). Also record which turn position produced the final-winning answer: sequential discussion may advantage the last speaker — if last-turn wins recur across debates, randomize or reverse turn order.
